The basics

Gambling is something you pay for, like a night out. It isn't a way to earn money, clear debts or win back what you've lost. Every bet carries a real chance of losing, and no system changes that.

This site is for adults 18 and over. If gambling is hurting your finances, relationships, work or health, stop and talk to someone. The services below are free.

Know the law

Australian law treats licensed wagering, such as sports and racing bets, very differently from online casino games. Online pokies, roulette, blackjack and other casino-style games can't legally be offered to people in Australia, even if the website works here.

Unlicensed operators may offer little protection: withdrawals can be delayed or refused, and some sites disappear with player balances. Check ACMA's register of licensed providers and its advice on avoiding illegal operators.

The credit ban

Licensed Australian online wagering providers can't accept credit cards or digital currency, and can't offer you credit. See ACMA's credit ban guidance. Whatever the payment method, only use money you can afford to lose.

Staying in control

Set your limits first

Decide on a money limit and a time limit before you start, and stop when you hit either one.

Protect essentials

Never gamble with rent, bills, food, health or savings money, and never borrow to gamble.

Keep a record

Track deposits, withdrawals and your total losses. The real figure is often a surprise.

Mind your state

Avoid gambling when you're stressed, upset, lonely or have been drinking.

Also worth doing

  • Use deposit limits, loss limits, session reminders and time-outs wherever they're offered.
  • Read the wagering, withdrawal and expiry rules before taking any bonus.
  • Turn off promotional emails and notifications that tempt you into unplanned bets.
  • Take regular breaks, and keep up the hobbies and friendships that have nothing to do with gambling.
  • Keep accounts, passwords and payment details to yourself, and never let anyone under 18 use them.

Warning signs

It may be time to stop and get support if you:

  • Spend more time or money than you meant to
  • Keep trying to win back losses
  • Hide gambling or lie about how much you spend
  • Borrow money or fall behind on bills
  • Feel restless or irritable when you can't gamble
  • Gamble to escape stress or low mood
  • Need bigger bets to feel the same buzz
  • Have tried to cut back and couldn't
  • Argue with family or friends about it

Anyone can be affected. Getting help early stops small problems turning into big ones.

BetStop: the National Self-Exclusion Register

BetStop is a free government service that lets you exclude yourself from every Australian-licensed online and phone wagering provider in one step. You can choose a period from three months to a lifetime.

While you're registered, those providers must stop you betting, can't open new accounts for you and can't send you marketing. They must also close your existing accounts and refund any balance.

What BetStop doesn't cover

Offshore online casinos, land-based casinos, pub and club pokies, and some state-run products. For those, use venue self-exclusion, bank gambling blocks and blocking software.

Register with BetStop or call 1800 238 786. You have to register yourself; no one else can do it on your behalf.

Extra steps that help

  • Ask your bank to block gambling transactions.
  • Delete gambling apps and saved card details.
  • Unsubscribe from gambling emails, texts and push alerts.
  • Install website or device blocking software.
  • Let someone you trust help manage money for a while.
  • Talk to a free financial counsellor about debts and bills.
  • Tell family or friends what you're doing so they can back you up.
  • Consider counselling before things reach a crisis.

Helping someone else

  • Pick a calm, private moment to talk.
  • Describe what you've noticed without blaming them.
  • Listen, and encourage them to get professional support.
  • Don't lend money for gambling or pay their debts without getting financial advice first.
  • Protect shared accounts and household money.
  • Look after yourself. The helplines above support family and friends too.

Reporting and complaints

Report a possibly illegal online gambling service through ACMA's online gambling complaints form.

If a licensed wagering provider lets a BetStop-registered person bet, open an account or receive marketing, complain through the BetStop contact page.

Only gamble with money you can afford to lose. If it stops being fun, stop and reach out.

Gambling stopped being fun? Call the National Gambling Helpline on 1800 858 858, free and open 24/7.
